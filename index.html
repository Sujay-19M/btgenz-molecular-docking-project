Executive summary

Five known AKT-targeting compounds (MK-2206, A-443654, Ipatasertib, Capivasertib and GSK690693) were docked against the AKT1 crystal structure 4EJN using AutoDock Vina, with Vinardo used for consensus ranking. All 5 of 5 dockings succeeded. The workflow ranked the compounds and combined the docking rank with drug-likeness, structural alerts and pose validity into a tier for each one.

MK-2206Strongest docking score in this dataset in every score set supplied (about -12.4 to -12.6 kcal/mol)
0.4878 ÅBest redocking RMSD reported. Validation: PASSED (VALIDATED_TOP_RANKED)
4 Tier A, 1 Tier BWorkflow tiers. Tier A means ready for molecular dynamics; Tier B means needs review

MK-2206 is the computationally prioritized compound and the workflow recommends it for molecular dynamics. It has a QED of 0.468, a LogP of 4.243 and no structural alerts. In the interaction report it shows 7 predicted interactions (1 hydrogen bond, 5 hydrophobic contacts and 1 salt bridge) involving residues ASP292, LEU210, LEU264, LYS268, TRP80 and TYR272. GSK690693 is Tier B because of a triple bond structural alert.

Please read the Data Consistency Notes. The supplied files contain three different sets of docking scores, and the order of Ipatasertib and Capivasertib differs between them. This report shows all three and does not choose between them. MK-2206 ranks first and A-443654 second in all three.

Protein-ligand interactions

Interactions were calculated by ProLIF from the docked poses. Values below come from the Interaction Report.

Metal complexes, halogen bonds and pi-cation interactions were zero for all five compounds. TRP80 appears for all five compounds and LYS268 for four of them, though whether a shared residue means a shared binding mode was not assessed in the source files.

ADMET and drug-likeness

The supplied campaign report lists five drug-likeness rule sets (Lipinski, Veber, Egan, Ghose and Muegge) and three structural alert filters (PAINS, Brenk and NIH). It reports the number of rule sets passed (out of 5) and the number of alerts, but not the individual result for each rule set.

The only alert reported is a triple_bond flag on GSK690693. Other ADMET properties such as solubility, permeability or toxicity were not part of the supplied files.

Validation

PASSEDDocking protocol validation
VALIDATED_TOP_RANKEDValidation route reported by the workflow
0.4878 ÅBest redocking RMSD

Redocking validation checks whether the docking protocol can reproduce a known reference binding pose under the chosen computational setup. An RMSD below about 2 Å is commonly treated as a successful reproduction. This is a check on the method. It does not show that any of the five compounds binds AKT1, and it is not experimental validation.

The campaign report also lists PoseBusters pose validity and rank confidence across seed replicates, but both columns are blank in the supplied report, so no values are shown. The top pose RMSD and the co-crystal ligand used for redocking are not named in the supplied files. The structure does contain the co-crystal ligand 0R4.

Data consistency notes

1. Three different docking score sets. The files disagree and the report does not decide which is correct. Score set A (Campaign report and the 10:08 export) and set B (Interaction report and the 10:17 export) each match a pair of files. Set C (ranked_results.csv) matches neither completely.
2. Rank order. Ipatasertib ranks 3rd and Capivasertib 4th in sets A and C. In set B, Capivasertib ranks 3rd and Ipatasertib 4th. The 10:17 export is stamped later than the 10:08 export, which suggests a rerun, but the files do not state this.
3. Interactions in the Campaign report. The campaign report lists interactions as "None" or "not measured" for every compound, while the Interaction Report provides counts. They may come from different runs. The interaction data here are attributed to the Interaction Report and to score set B.
4. Ligand coordinates. The ligands in the SDF are centered at the coordinate origin, so they are input conformers, not docked poses in the protein frame.

Limitations

  • Docking is a computational method. Docking scores are not experimental binding affinities.
  • Predicted interactions come from computational analysis of predicted poses.
  • Drug-likeness and ADMET values are computational predictions.
  • Results depend on the input structure and on the computational settings used.
  • Experimental validation may be required before drawing conclusions about activity.

Not available in the supplied files

Docked pose coordinates, P2Rank pocket data, Excel workbooks, PoseBusters and replicate confidence values, per-rule drug-likeness results, software versions, run dates and file hashes.
